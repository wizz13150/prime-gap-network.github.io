First principles

Two primes.
Nothing prime between.

A prime is an integer greater than 1 with exactly two positive divisors: 1 and itself. The first few are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, and 13. A number greater than 1 that is not prime is composite.

If p and q are consecutive primes, their gap is g = q − p. Every integer strictly between them is composite.

23 → 24, 25, 26, 27, 28 → 29GAP = 29 − 23 = 6 · FIVE INTERIOR COMPOSITES

Be careful with conventions: some older sources count the composite integers instead of the endpoint difference. Here, “gap” always means q − p. Except for 2 → 3, prime gaps are even because every prime after 2 is odd.

Source: PrimePages, The Gaps Between Primes — including its composite-count convention.

A scale for comparison

Length tells you how far.
Merit adds where.

Primes become sparser on average as numbers grow. The prime number theorem gives a local average spacing of approximately ln(p), where ln is the natural logarithm.

merit = (q − p) / ln(p)FOR CONSECUTIVE PRIMES p < q

For 113 → 127, this is 14 / ln(113) ≈ 2.961. The gap is about three times the local average scale. Merit helps compare gaps at different magnitudes; it is neither a probability nor a proof of a record.

Source: Thomas R. Nicely’s original table rubric, preserved by the Prime Gap List Project.

Three claims worth separating

First known occurrence: the earliest example currently recorded for a gap size. There might still be an undiscovered earlier example.

First occurrence: the earliest actual example of that exact size, established with the necessary coverage below it.

Maximal gap: a gap longer than every preceding gap. At 113 the gap is 14; the later gap of 10 at 139 is a first occurrence of size 10, but is not maximal.

Source: OEIS A005250, record gaps; see also lower endpoints in A002386.

How a search
becomes evidence.

A fast GPU can explore an interval. A useful scientific result also needs a precise question, trustworthy arithmetic, and a record of what was covered.

  1. [a, b)

    Declare the job

    Fix the interval, minimum gap, software version, and boundary policy. A half-open interval includes a and excludes b.

  2. × 2, 3, 5…

    Remove composites

    A sieve crosses out multiples of known primes. At large magnitudes, its survivors are candidates, not automatically primes.

  3. p → q

    Search the survivors

    Primality screening and a gap-search strategy locate promising intervals. A search cutoff changes which gaps are eligible to be reported.

  4. ✓ p · ∅ · ✓ q

    Verify the finding

    Establish prime endpoints and a composite interior with methods valid in the stated domain. Preserve evidence for each claim.

  5. ▤ + [a, b)

    Account for coverage

    Retain checkpoints and completed segments. Check boundaries and omissions before making a coverage or first-occurrence claim.

One threshold is not every threshold.

Checking a range for gaps of at least 100 does not establish the first occurrence of a gap of length 10. A partial run also cannot stand in for its unfinished interval. Compare like-for-like policies before combining coverage.

Method and evidence context: Nicely’s table rubric, Seth Troisi’s combined-sieve paper, and Scout’s documented proof and coverage limits. This diagram is an explanatory workflow, not a certification of an implementation.

A discovery has
more than one obligation.

“Found,” “proved,” and “exhaustively searched” describe different evidence. A reliable research record says which one it has earned.

01 / ENDPOINTS

Are both ends prime?

Passing a probable-prime test is not automatically a primality proof. The report needs the method and the domain in which its conclusion is valid.

02 / INTERIOR

Is the whole gap empty?

Every interior integer must be composite. A hidden prime would split the interval and invalidate the claimed consecutive-prime gap.

03 / COVERAGE

What else was searched?

A local gap can be correct without being a first occurrence. That stronger claim depends on verified earlier coverage and compatible search thresholds.

Exhaustive search is a coverage claim.

Every admissible position in the declared interval must be accounted for under a stated method and threshold. Boundary halos matter: a gap can cross a ticket or chunk edge. A fast run, a saved checkpoint, or proved endpoints alone do not establish exhaustive coverage.

Further reading: Prime Gap List Project FAQ and Scout’s documented exactness and coverage limits.

Why keep looking?

Data to study.
Claims to test.

Computational searches document examples, extend tables, and help test predictions about prime spacing within finite ranges. They also expose practical questions about reproducible arithmetic and distributed verification.

Large-gap theory asks for statements that hold at unbounded scales. For example, the work of Ford, Green, Konyagin, and Tao gives lower bounds on how large some gaps must be. A finite search and an asymptotic theorem answer different questions.

Source: Ford, Green, Konyagin & Tao, Large gaps between consecutive prime numbers (2016).

Search algorithms are also research objects. Seth Troisi’s Combined Sieve Algorithm for Prime Gaps describes sharing sieve work across many candidate intervals. It is useful background on a targeted search method; it does not establish the correctness of a different PGN or Scout implementation.

Source: Seth Troisi, Combined Sieve Algorithm for Prime Gaps (2020).
