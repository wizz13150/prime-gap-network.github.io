A practical contribution guide

Small changes.
Clear evidence.

Begin in the PGN Discord for community discussion, or in the relevant repository for a reproducible software or website issue.

  1. Choose the right place

    Website copy, links, and accessibility belong in the website repository. Scout behavior belongs in Scout. PGN concepts and coordination belong in the project discussion. Keep unrelated changes out of a report.

  2. Give someone a way to reproduce it

    Include the release or commit, operating system, exact steps, expected result, and observed result. For numerical issues, preserve exact integers and search policy. Remove private paths or identifying data before posting logs.

  3. Keep the claim narrow

    Say whether evidence is a candidate, a proved endpoint, a checked gap, or verified coverage. Attach the method and its boundary. Never turn a local benchmark into a network-scale claim.

  4. Contribute a reviewable change

    For this buildless website, edit the page generator or assets, regenerate pages, and run the included checks. Cite original sources and label proposals. See CONTRIBUTING.md in the repository for commands.

Useful questions
for a protocol review.

The working design is explicit enough to ask concrete questions. The answers need specifications and tests, not just confidence.

  • What stops a participant from tailoring a report after learning expected audit counts?
  • Do the two audits cover ten genuinely distinct partitions?
  • What information is bound by each commitment, and when is it revealed?
  • Can colluding participants satisfy a check without doing the intended work?
  • What survives restart between each pair of protocol transitions?
  • Which work remains pending when only one or two miners are available?
  • How are cross-ticket gap boundaries and incomplete work handled?
  • Does measured capacity include audit, verification, recovery, and communication costs?

Questions, answered plainly.

Current status and terminology, without a launch narrative. Last reviewed 31 October 2026.

Is PGN live, and can I mine coins now?

PGN Horizon, core, and network are a testbed under construction. This website announces no production launch, real coins, wallet, exchange listing, or production explorer. Reward and consensus rules remain design and testing work.

Who conceived Prime Gap Network?

Rob Smith, known as robert44444uk. The public PGN and website repositories explicitly credit his concept and initiative. Supporting software and community infrastructure are collaborative work.

Is Prime Gap Scout a PGN mode?

No. Scout is a separate standalone NVIDIA CUDA search tool. It does not require a wallet, pool, or blockchain connection. Its local ranges do not become network tickets.

Do I manually reserve a PGN range?

Not in Robert’s current working network design: an authority randomly issues tickets from a rolling pool. Historical community reservations and independent Scout search coordination are separate; consult their maintainers rather than assuming this website reserves anything.

Does Scout run on a CPU or OpenCL device?

The shipped search path described here is NVIDIA CUDA. CPU and OpenCL support are planned, not shipped. Check official release documentation for supported platforms and device constraints.

Does self-audit count as independent verification?

No. Repeating a calculation can catch errors, but it is not independent participation when the same actor performs it. The proposed network needs an explicit policy for operating with one or two miners without overstating independence.

What does Gold mean, and what is it worth?

Gold is a working qualifying-result category in the proposed reward model. Verified Gold closes an epoch in that model. Exact qualification, reward allocation, and retarget rules are still under design; no monetary value or payout is promised.

Will one Gold always arrive every ten minutes?

No. Roughly one verified Gold per ten minutes is a proposed difficulty objective based on measured capacity. It is not a guaranteed interval, measured current behavior, or an individual miner’s expected reward.

Are PGN and Gapcoin the same project?

No. Gapcoin is a separate project with its own software, chain, and documented history. Historical links in the library are background reading. They do not transfer Gapcoin’s supply, launch, releases, or results to PGN.

Does a high-merit gap prove a new record?

No. A record claim needs the appropriate endpoint and interior evidence, comparison with maintained tables, and any required coverage evidence. A first known occurrence and a proved first occurrence are different claims.

Do I need a GPU to help?

No. Explain a concept, review the proposed protocol, improve an English guide, check sources, report an accessibility problem, or help maintain accurate scientific records.

Where are live network statistics?

This draft has no live network data source. It deliberately displays no invented miner counts, hashrates, production throughput, rewards, or search-coverage counters. The homepage figure is a labelled small-number mathematical example.
