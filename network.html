02 / The network

Coordinate the search.
Earn the confidence.

PGN is exploring how a community can assign useful computational work, verify it, and preserve a durable research record. Horizon, core, and network remain a testbed.

Current statusTicketsAuditsSmall networksGold & epochsOpen decisions
Working proposal · not a production specification

The protocol concepts on this page reflect Rob Smith’s working design, supplied for this draft’s review on 31 October 2026. They are design inputs, not independently verified production behavior. The public project README is earlier and less specific. There is no live coin, production explorer, launch date, or demonstrated 100,000-miner capacity announced here.

The purpose

Make work traceable
from assignment
to acceptance.

PGN aims to connect coordinated prime-gap searches with explicit verification. A ticket says what to search. A committed report fixes what the miner claims. Audits check the work before it is accepted.

“Horizon” refers to experimental work around the PGN testbed. This website is an explanation and community entry point; it does not issue tickets, reserve ranges, accept reports, or operate a wallet.

Source: PGN public project presentation — early development and community discussion.

01 / Assignment

Tickets from
a rolling pool.

2 × 1014

Integer positions per proposed ticket.
A design parameter, not a performance claim.

In Robert’s working model, an authority randomly issues tickets from a rolling pool of 10,000. The pool begins at 1.5 × 1020; each ticket covers 2 × 1014 positions.

Random assignment is the proposed coordination mechanism. Participants do not manually reserve PGN tickets. A ticket’s identity, exact interval, and state need to stay attached to its reports and subsequent verification.

Historical range records are a separate matter.

Independent Scout searches and existing community reservations do not become PGN tickets. Check the relevant maintainers’ current records before independently choosing work that might duplicate a coordinated search. This site does not replace those records.

A report is a claim.
Acceptance comes later.

The proposed order matters: reports and auditor commitments must be fixed before information that could let participants tailor their answers is revealed.

Commit before the checks

The miner commits its report before two audits covering ten distinct partitions. Expected counts stay hidden until auditor commitments have been made. Commitments are intended to bind answers before comparison; the commitment format and implementation still require specification and review.

Pending means pending

Work is accepted only after the required verification. Pending states must survive restart so an interrupted process cannot silently discard an audit obligation or transform unverified work into accepted work.

These are intended invariants of the design. They need failure tests, recovery tests, and adversarial review before production claims are justified.

A real design constraint

Start small.
Be honest about trust.

The network needs a useful way to operate with only one or two miners. That does not make an audit by the original worker independent.

A small deployment must explicitly say which checks are independent, which are repeated locally, which rely on an authority, and which remain pending. The exact bootstrap policy is still a testing and governance question.

Smartnodes or dedicated heavy-compute nodes are an optional last resort in this design discussion. They are not a promised requirement or a substitute for documenting the trust model.

02 / Epoch design

Verified Gold
closes an epoch.

In the proposed reward model, a verified Gold closes a reward epoch. “Gold” is a working classification of qualifying work; this draft does not define an unapproved mathematical threshold or promise a payout.

The difficulty objective is roughly one Gold per ten minutes, calibrated from measured capacity. This is a design target. It is not a fixed promise for an individual miner or evidence of current network throughput.

Exact Gold qualification, reward allocation, retarget rules, and the handling of delayed verification remain under design and testing.

Questions still on the bench.

A useful roadmap states the decisions and evidence still needed. It does not turn a proposal into a launch announcement.
